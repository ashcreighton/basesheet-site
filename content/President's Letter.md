Greetings,

As we move through another Arizona summer, one challenge rises above all others for those who work outdoors: protecting our workforce from the dangers of extreme heat.

Every year, Arizona's temperatures remind us that heat is far more than an inconvenience—it is a serious workplace hazard. Roofers, forklift operators, manufacturing and plant personnel, construction workers, and countless others perform essential jobs under some of the harshest environmental conditions in the country. Heat-related illnesses can develop rapidly and, if left untreated, become life-threatening.

As an industry, we have a responsibility to ensure that every employee returns home safely at the end of each day. Safety is not simply a company policy—it is a commitment we make to our employees, their families, and the communities we serve.

Education remains our most effective tool. Every employee should recognize the warning signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke, understand the importance of staying hydrated, take scheduled rest breaks, and never hesitate to speak up when they or a coworker begin experiencing symptoms. Supervisors and crew leaders play a vital role by monitoring their teams, encouraging hydration, adjusting work schedules when possible, and fostering a culture where safety always takes priority over production.

Simple actions truly save lives:

  - Drink water frequently throughout the day—don't wait until you feel thirsty.
  - Take regular breaks in shaded or air-conditioned areas.
  - Wear lightweight, breathable clothing and appropriate sun protection.
  - Watch for signs of heat illness in yourself and your coworkers, including dizziness, confusion, nausea, heavy sweating, or the absence of sweating.
  - Seek immediate medical attention if heat stroke is suspected.

I have always appreciated what makes Arizona such an incredible place to live, including our beautiful sunshine and warm climate. But even those of us who enjoy the heat need an occasional reminder that extreme temperatures demand our respect. The consequences of heat-related illness are real, and they can affect anyone.

I encourage every company to take a few moments each day to remind employees that temperatures are rising and to stay hydrated. A simple conversation during a morning safety meeting or tailgate talk can make a lasting difference.

The Arizona Roofing Contractors Association remains committed to providing our members with safety education, training, and resources that help prevent injuries and protect workers from heat-related hazards. By working together, we can continue building a culture where safety is everyone's responsibility and every employee has the knowledge and confidence to recognize and respond to dangerous conditions.

As temperatures continue to climb, I encourage each company to review its Heat Illness Prevention Program, reinforce daily safety meetings, and ensure every employee has access to water, shade, and rest. These straightforward measures can mean the difference between a productive workday and a medical emergency.

Thank you for your continued commitment to safety, professionalism, and protecting Arizona's workforce. Together, we can make this summer a safe one for everyone working under our Arizona sun.

Stay safe, stay hydrated, and look out for one another.

Tracy Wallis
