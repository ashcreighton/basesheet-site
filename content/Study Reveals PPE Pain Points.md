The 2026 PPE Pain Points Study found that more than two-thirds of employers struggle to ensure new workers consistently follow proper personal protective equipment protocols, according to *Safety+Health* magazine.

The research was conducted jointly by the International Safety Equipment Association and J.J. Keller & Associates Inc., surveying 500 employers about significant challenges and trends related to PPE usage.

Nearly 70% of survey participants worked in the manufacturing, construction and transportation sectors. Key findings revealed that workers neglect PPE because they "do not want to wear it (56%); do not think it is necessary (48%); and believe it makes the job more difficult (38%)."

When selecting PPE, more than 80% of respondents prioritized comfort, followed by durability (79%) and cost (66%). About 38% reported that sizing presented challenges, particularly for female workers.

The survey also found that 10% of respondents extended PPE usage duration or adopted lower protection levels because of tariffs. ISEA cautioned that reducing PPE use by 10% could result in 143 additional workplace fatalities, approximately 47,000 more injuries and $2.2 billion in economic losses.

NRCA directs employers to its health and safety resources for worker protection guidance.
