Companies frequently encounter hiring disappointments despite overall success in recruiting quality staff. Poor hiring decisions can drain time, money and resources, making it valuable to identify warning signs during the recruitment process.

According to an *Inc.* article by Dave Kerpen, CEO of Kerpen Ventures, three key indicators suggest a candidate may become a problematic hire.

The first is when applicants consistently speak poorly of previous employers, describing supervisors as "toxic" and teams as "dysfunctional." This pattern may reveal more about the candidate than about their former workplace. Kerpen notes that while legitimate workplace problems exist, strong candidates acknowledge difficulties while taking "ownership for their part and speak with grace about the people they left behind."

The second is an inability to discuss personal failures, which demonstrates weak self-awareness. Kerpen emphasizes that ideal hires can discuss concrete setbacks — failed launches, unsuccessful hires or missed targets — and explain what they'd do differently. He identifies a "willingness to be honest about a real failure" as the strongest predictor of career potential.

The third is a lack of genuine interest in the organization, role or industry. Strong candidates arrive with thoughtful questions about customers, product strategy and management approach, while weak candidates ask only about compensation and time off. Kerpen asserts that curiosity "is the leading indicator of how high a person will rise."
